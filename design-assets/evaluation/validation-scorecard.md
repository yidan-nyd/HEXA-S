# HEXA-S Validation Scorecard

| Dimension | Question | Example evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeatability | Does equal stretch produce a similar response? | Cycle error, variance |
| Hysteresis and drift | Does loading or time shift the baseline? | Drift and recovery time |
| Environmental robustness | Do climate, washing, and donning change output? | Condition tests, recalibration rate |
| Spatial mapping | Does each channel remain tied to its region? | Isolated-region test, crosstalk |
| Geometric validity | Does relative signal correspond to physical change? | Comparison with scan, gauge, or fixture |
| Real-time performance | Is capture-to-display stable? | Frame rate, latency, packet loss |
| Wear experience | Is it comfortable and usable? | Comfort score, pressure points, donning time |
| Data-quality visibility | Are faults detected and explained? | Fault detection and recovery rate |
| Pattern recognition | Are repeated trends found without alert overload? | Sensitivity, specificity, false alerts, calibration |
| Comprehension | Do people understand evidence and limits? | Correct explanation, misunderstanding cases |
| Clinical usefulness | Does it improve judgement rather than add burden? | Task time, consistency, interviews |
| Human control | Can people edit, reject, pause, and take over? | Takeover success, audit trail |
| Privacy | Is data used within consent? | Access, retention, deletion tests |
| Outcome | Does it improve comfort or fitting? | Discomfort, adjustment count, longitudinal feedback |

## Recommended validation order

1. Material and sensor testing on controlled fixtures.
2. Engineering validation of the wearable and electronics.
3. Usability work with appropriate ethics and oversight.
4. Exploratory studies with prosthetic users and prosthetists.
5. Prospective validation after the algorithm is locked.
6. Product assessment under applicable regulation, risk management, and quality systems.
